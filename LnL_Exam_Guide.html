Grade 12 External Examination Preparation Guide

GUIDE TO THE LANGUAGE AND LITERATURE OBJECTIVE PAPER 2

As a grade 12 student, you need to know the specific sections of your examination and know the guidelines for answering each type of question. This guide covers the essential strategies for success in your Language and Literature Objective Paper 2.

Guide to Multiple Choice Questions

Multiple choice questions test your understanding of key concepts. To answer them effectively:

1. Read the Question Carefully

Before looking at the options, understand what the question is asking. Identify keywords that guide your answer.

2. Eliminate Wrong Answers

Start by eliminating options you know are incorrect. This narrows down your choices and increases your chances of selecting the correct answer.

Example:

What is the chemical symbol for the element hydrogen?

a) Hg
b) He
c) H
d) Na

By eliminating obviously wrong answers, you can quickly see that option "c) H" is correct.

The Cloze Section Guide

The Cloze Test is a common yet high-scoring question type. It requires you to fill in missing words in a passage using contextual clues.

Strategies for Cloze Tests:

1. Read the Entire Passage First – Understand the overall meaning and context before filling in blanks.

2. Use Context Clues – Look at surrounding words and sentences to predict the missing word.

3. Consider Grammar – Think about word types (nouns, verbs, adjectives) that fit grammatically in each blank.

4. Check Your Work – After completing the cloze test, read through it again to ensure all words make sense.

Example of a Cloze Test:

Passage: "Plants are living organisms that _______ sunlight to produce energy. This process is called _______."

Answers: use, photosynthesis

Short Answer Questions Guide

1. Understand What is Being Asked

Pay close attention to command words such as:

Define – Give a precise meaning.
Explain – Provide reasons or describe how something works.
Describe – Give details about something.
Analyze – Break down and examine components.

2. Provide Specific Examples

When answering short answer questions, always support your answer with examples from the passage or text.

Example Question:

Describe the process of photosynthesis.

Answer: Photosynthesis is the process where plants use sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide to produce glucose and oxygen. It occurs in chloroplasts and provides energy for plant growth and survival.

Essay Questions Guide

1. Plan Before Writing

Take time to outline your essay before writing. Include:

Introduction: State your thesis or main argument.
Body: Provide evidence and examples to support your points.
Conclusion: Summarize your ideas and restate the main point.

2. Support Claims with Evidence

Every claim you make should be backed up by evidence from the text. Use direct quotes when appropriate.

Example Question:

Discuss the impact of social media on society.

Introduction: Social media has transformed communication and interaction in modern society. This essay explores the impact of social media on communication, mental health, and social relationships.

Body Paragraph 1: Positive impacts include increased connectivity and access to information. Social media platforms allow people to stay connected across distances.

Body Paragraph 2: Negative impacts include issues with privacy, cyberbullying, and mental health concerns related to social comparison.

Conclusion: While social media offers benefits, it is important to use it responsibly and be aware of potential negative consequences.

Problem-Solving and Quantitative Questions

Read the Problem Carefully

Identify what information is given and what you need to find.

Example:

A train leaves Station A at 8:00 am and travels at 80 km/h towards Station B, which is 400 km away. Another train leaves Station B at 9:00 am, travelling towards Station A at 100 km/h. When will the trains meet?

Solution: Set up equations based on distance = speed × time. Solve to find the meeting point.

Reading Comprehension Guide

Reading comprehension questions assess your ability to understand, analyze, and interpret written texts. Here are key strategies:

1. Preview the Questions First

Before reading the passage, glance at the questions. This helps you focus on relevant information while reading.

2. Identify Paragraph Roles

Understanding the function of each paragraph helps you navigate the passage:

Claim – introduces an idea or argument.

Evidence – supports the claim with facts, examples, or reasoning.

Objection – presents a counterpoint or alternative view.

Response – addresses the objection and reinforces the claim.

Tracking these roles helps you see how the argument develops.

3. Read Actively

Active reading improves comprehension and interpretation:

Underline key points (names, dates, facts).
Circle difficult vocabulary.
Write marginal notes to track your thoughts.
Mark the main idea in each paragraph.

4. Make Inferences

Some questions require you to infer information not directly stated in the text. Use context clues and logical reasoning to draw conclusions.

Inference is essential for higher-level comprehension questions.

6. Use Evidence to Support Answers

When answering questions:

State your answer clearly.
Cite the exact part of the passage that supports your answer.
Use quotes when appropriate to strengthen your response.

This prevents guesswork and ensures accuracy.

7. Watch Out for Common Traps

Extreme language ("always", "never") is often incorrect.

Outside knowledge should not influence your answer — rely on the passage.

Be careful with questions that ask "which is NOT" or similar negations.

Guide to Interpreting Proverbs and Idioms

Proverbs and idioms are figurative expressions that convey deeper meanings than their literal words suggest. Understanding them is important for language comprehension exams.

What Are Proverbs?

Proverbs are short, traditional sayings that express wisdom, advice, or observations about life. Examples include:

"The early bird catches the worm" – Those who act quickly gain advantages.
"Actions speak louder than words" – What you do matters more than what you say.
"A stitch in time saves nine" – Addressing problems early prevents bigger issues.

What Are Idioms?

Idioms are phrases or expressions whose meanings cannot be understood from the individual words alone. Examples include:

"Break the ice" – Start a conversation or social interaction.
"Burn the midnight oil" – Work late into the night.
"Hit the books" – Study hard.

Strategies for Interpreting Proverbs and Idioms

1. Look for Context – The surrounding text often provides clues to meaning.

2. Consider the Speaker's Intent – Why is this proverb or idiom being used? What point is being made?

3. Relate to Universal Themes – Proverbs often connect to common human experiences like hard work, wisdom, and consequences.

These proverbs are commonly used in conversation, writing, and media to convey wisdom, advice, or cultural insights, making them valuable for both native speakers and English Learners.

Guide To Interpreting Cartoon Strips

To interpret cartoon strips accurately in an exam, consider the following strategies:

1. Identify Key Elements

Look for symbols, exaggeration, and labels that convey messages. Understand the cartoon's perspective and the artist's intent.

2. Analyze Visuals

Examine the colors, size, and layout of the cartoon. Ask questions about the use of colors, exaggeration, and the overall message.

3. Practice Analysis

Engage in exercises that require you to analyze cartoons and comic strips. This can include identifying characters, their expressions, and the captions that accompany them.

4. Use Context

Understand the context in which the cartoon is presented. This can help you interpret the cartoon's message more accurately.

5. Review Techniques

Familiarize yourself with techniques such as satire, irony, and exaggeration, which are common in cartoons.

By applying these strategies, you can enhance your ability to interpret cartoon strips and improve your reading comprehension skills in exams.

Guide to Interpreting Reading and Comprehension Texts Accurately in an Exam

To accurately interpret reading and comprehension texts in an exam, follow these strategies:

1. Preview

Spend 20 to 30 seconds scanning the text to build a mental framework and activate prior knowledge, improving retention.

2. Identify Passage Type

Different passages require different approaches. Recognizing the structure helps predict question types.

3. Focus on Main Idea

After reading the first paragraph, ask what the author is trying to prove or explain. Practice summarizing each paragraph in one sentence.

4. Track Keywords and Transition Signals

Words like "these" and "These" indicate shifts in logic, while transition words signal important arguments or counterarguments.

5. Manage Time Wisely

Avoid subvocalization (silent lip movement) to increase reading speed without losing accuracy. By implementing these strategies, you can enhance your reading comprehension skills and perform better in exams.

Additional Guidelines for Reading and Comprehension Questions in a Competitive Examination

When tackling reading comprehension questions in competitive examinations, it's essential to follow these guidelines to ensure accuracy and efficiency:

1. Identify the Question Type

Determine whether the question is fact-based, inference, vocabulary, or main idea. This helps in selecting the right approach to answering.

2. Scan the Questions

Quickly scan the questions to understand what information is needed before reading the passage.

3. Read Actively

Read the passage with specific questions in mind, underlining or mentally noting the sections that relate to the questions.

4. Answer Directly

For direct fact questions, use the passage text directly. Refer back to the passage for support.

5. Understand Inference Questions

For inference questions, use logic and textual evidence to draw reasonable conclusions that are not explicitly stated.

6. Manage Your Time

Allocate your time wisely between reading and answering questions. Do not spend too much time on difficult questions; move on and return later if time permits.

By following these comprehensive guidelines and practicing regularly, you will build the skills necessary to excel in reading and comprehension sections of your Language and Literature examination.
